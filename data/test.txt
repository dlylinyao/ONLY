Russian neo-Nazi militant Voislav Torden is back in court, appealing a life sentence handed down last year by Helsinki District Court for war crimes in Ukraine. The Helsinki Court of Appeal began hearing the case on Monday.

Last March, the District Court found Torden guilty of four war crimes. They stemmed from an ambush in the Luhansk region of eastern Ukraine in September 2014 and the events that followed. Twenty-two Ukrainian soldiers were killed in the attack.

Torden has denied all charges.

Ukrainian soldiers ambushed

According to the district court, Torden (formerly known as Yan Petrovsky) served as deputy commander of the Rusich militant group. Rusich was fighting in eastern Ukraine alongside Russian troops and pro-Moscow separatists. The BBC describes Rusich as a subunit of the Wagner group, while the New York Times calls it a neo-Nazi militia group.

According to the prosecutor, Rusich soldiers deceived Ukrainian soldiers in September 2014 by hanging a Ukrainian flag at a checkpoint. When the Ukrainians stopped, Rusich fighters opened fire, the indictment says.

According to the district court, it was clear that Rusich participated in the armed attack against the Ukrainian soldiers. However, according to the court, it was not proven that it was Rusich who was behind the ambush. The court noted that there were other groups and individuals at the scene.

According to the prosecutor, Torden led the actions of Rusich's soldiers when they shot at least four wounded Ukrainian soldiers after the ambush. The district court said there was evidence that the Rusich combatants had killed at least one soldier, but that there was insufficient evidence regarding the others.

Torden was also convicted on charges including the mutilation of a wounded Ukrainian soldier and the distribution of "degrading" images of a dead soldier.

Russia dismisses "show trial"

Torden has denied holding a leadership position within Rusich. According to his defence, Torden had no military training. It claims that his main tasks were creating propaganda and recruitment videos and obtaining humanitarian aid.

Torden said he went to Luhansk in 2014 because he had heard that the Russian-speaking population there was being oppressed.

Last spring, the Russian Embassy in Helsinki alleged the verdict Torden's conviction had been politicised, and that the "show trial" was part of Western hybrid warfare against Russia.

Russia's news agency, Tass, reported that Russian diplomats in Finland "are closely monitoring the situation and are prepared to protect Torden's rights".

Earlier Russia’s Federal Agency for the Commonwealth of Independent States Affairs, Compatriots Living Abroad, and International Humanitarian Cooperation said it was "outraged by the hostile and illegal actions of the Finnish authorities" in the case.

Detained at Helsinki Airport before flight to Nice

Torden arrived in Finland from Russia in July 2023 by car via the Vaalimaa border crossing point. Shortly after crossing the border, the Border Guard found out that he had been previously known as Jan Petrovsky and that he had an entry ban imposed by Norway and the Czech Republic for the entire Schengen area.

The next day, Torden was detained at Helsinki Airport just before boarding a flight to Nice, France. At first, Finnish authorities investigated him for violating the entry ban, but then found him on an EU sanctions list.

In late 2023, the Finnish Supreme Court ruled that Torden could not be extradited to Ukraine due to the country's poor prison conditions.

It ruled that he should be tried in Finland for war crimes based on international treaties signed by Finland. Finnish courts have previously prosecuted cases relating to massacres in Iraq and Rwanda.

In late 2024, Rusich claimed that it had an agreement with the Russian Border Guard to carry out intelligence and surveillance tasks along the Finnish border near the Saimaa Canal.
